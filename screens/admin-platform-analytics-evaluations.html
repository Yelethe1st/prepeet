Reviewer agreement by tenant

How often the reviewer's recorded decision matched the suggested band, last 30 days.

Overrides are expected and healthy — they are not errors. Prepeet produces evidence and a suggested band; the hiring decision belongs to the reviewer, who also sees a CV, a portfolio, a registration number or a reference the transcript never contained. A tenant at 100% agreement would worry us more than one at 85%: it would suggest reviewers were rubber-stamping. What we watch is a sudden change in a tenant's rate, or overrides clustered in one competency.

Band distribution by role family

Share of evaluations in each suggested band, by the role family the rubric belongs to.

Read this chart with care. Differences between role families reflect two things only: how each rubric is written, and who applied to those roles in the last 30 days. Nursing and teaching rubrics use tighter, more scripted competency definitions than software; sales roles attract a broader applicant pool. None of this is a statement about groups of people. Do not use this panel to compare the ability of nurses with the ability of engineers, and do not export it into a report that implies you can. If a band distribution shifts sharply, investigate the rubric change that caused it — start with Rubrics.

This comparison is observational, not an experiment. v4.3 was rolled out to Northwind Health and Orbital Labs first — two tenants with longer sessions, more experienced reviewers and different role mixes from the four still on v4.2. Nobody was randomised. The 3.3-point agreement gap is consistent with the rubric being better, and equally consistent with those two tenants simply being easier to agree on. Treat it as a reason to run a proper split rollout, not as proof.
