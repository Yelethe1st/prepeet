Band anchors

One description per band, per competency. Anchors describe what the answer contained — never what kind of person the candidate is.

Evidence sufficiency rules

The minimum amount of material an evaluation needs before it is allowed to produce a score.

Coverage is the share of the role's competencies that reached their minimum evidence.

In plain English: a competency with fewer than its minimum number of evidence spans is reported as Insufficient evidence rather than given a number, and if fewer than 70% of competencies clear their minimum the evaluation shows no overall band at all — the recruiter sees the transcript and the partial competencies instead.

    How to write an anchor

    Anchors describe observable behaviours in the answer — what the candidate said, the specifics they gave, the sequence they described, whether they checked understanding. They must never describe personal traits, attitude, confidence, accent, background or supposed cultural fit. If an anchor cannot be evidenced by quoting the transcript, it is not an anchor: rewrite it until a reviewer could point at the sentence that satisfies it.

    Prepeet produces a competency signal from evidence. It does not decide outcomes, and a published rubric does not remove a reviewer's obligation to read the transcript.
