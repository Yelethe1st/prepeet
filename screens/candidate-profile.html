Situation
Halcyon's consignment status was rebuilt by a nightly batch, so customers phoned in about parcels the website said had not moved for hours.
Task
I owned the tracking domain and was asked to get status lag under a minute without a rewrite freeze on the rest of the platform.
Action
I moved scan events onto a stream, kept the batch running in parallel for six weeks and diffed the two outputs nightly so we could prove the stream matched before switching reads over.
Result
Lag went from about 6 hours to under 40 seconds. Tracking-related contact volume dropped by a third in the quarter after launch.
Practise this

Situation
Over one weekend, 1,400 stock reconciliation jobs re-ran and created duplicate purchase orders across 240 stores.
Task
I was second on call and picked it up at 06:20 on the Sunday.
Action
I started from the symptom rather than the code: compared order timestamps against job start times, found the retry had no idempotency key, stopped the scheduler, then wrote a reversal script that only touched orders with an identical line-item hash.
Result
Reversed 1,382 duplicates before stores opened on Monday. The remaining 18 had already been received, so we handled those with the supplier by hand. Idempotency keys went in the following sprint.
Practise this

Situation
The platform lead wanted the pricing monolith split into seven services. I thought three was right and that seven would leave us with distributed transactions we could not staff.
Task
Reach a decision the four consuming teams could actually live with, without turning it into a stand-off.
Action
I wrote up both options with the specific call paths that would cross a network boundary in each, then asked each consuming team which of those calls they would have to make synchronously. Three of the four flagged the same two boundaries.
Result
We landed on three services. The platform lead reviewed the doc and changed position on the basis of the call-path table, not because I argued harder.
Practise this

Situation
At Kettlewell our integration tests caught that a patient's second telephone number was being written into the next-of-kin field for one trust's EHR profile.
Task
Nobody owned the integration end to end. I had found it, so I took it.
Action
I paused the pilot's writes myself before asking permission, wrote up the affected record count, and sat in the trust's clinical safety call to explain it in their language rather than ours. Then I fixed the mapping and added a schema assertion so it could not recur silently.
Result
41 records were affected and all were corrected within two days. The trust kept the pilot running. I still run the schema-assertion pattern on every integration I build.
Practise this
