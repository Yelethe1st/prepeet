Voice by surface

Three audiences read three different products out of the same evaluation. The tone rule is not decoration — it is how we stop a practice tool sounding like a verdict and a hiring tool sounding like an oracle.

Candidate — supportive and direct

Second person. Name the gap, then name the next action. Never soften a gap into meaninglessness, never imply the product decides anything about their career.

Third person about the candidate, imperative about the reviewer's job. Every claim is traceable to a quoted span. The reviewer decides; Prepeet supplies material.

The never-claim list

Prepeet records what a candidate said and scores it against a published rubric. It does not read minds, feelings or futures. These four claims are prohibited in every surface — product copy, marketing, PDF exports, emails and error messages alike. Each row below shows the banned phrasing and the rewrite that says the true thing.

Prohibited claims and their compliant rewrites
Never claimBanned phrasingCompliant rewriteWhy
Personality Strong culture fit · assertive personality type Gave three examples of challenging a senior decision, two with a named outcome. A 40-minute interview is not a personality instrument, and personality is not a job requirement.
Emotion Candidate appeared anxious · low confidence detected · sentiment negative Answered in 9 seconds with no worked reasoning. Two follow-ups were needed to reach specifics. We measure speech content and timing, not affect. Inferring emotion from voice discriminates by accent, disability and culture.
Honesty Possible fabrication · candidate may be exaggerating Two statements conflict on who owned the escalation. Both spans are marked contradiction for the reviewer to weigh. We can show two spans that disagree. We cannot know which is true or why they differ.
Guaranteed future performance 92% likely to succeed in this role · predicted top performer Systems design 78 out of 100 against the Senior Backend Engineer rubric, from six evidence spans. This is a practice signal, not a prediction of hiring outcomes. No validation study supports an outcome prediction, and stating one invites both a legal claim and a bad hire.
Vocabulary. Use: evidence, what the candidate said, competency signal, confidence, coverage, insufficient evidence, reviewer decision. Avoid: personality fit, confidence detected, sentiment, culture score, AI decides, verified, guaranteed.

3 · Theming

One attribute drives everything: data-theme="light" or data-theme="dark". Because the selectors in tokens.css are attribute selectors rather than :root-only, the attribute works on any element — which is how the two panels in the previous section render both themes on one page. Use that for documentation and previews; never for production layout, because nested themes break color-scheme and form-control rendering.

How a page picks its theme

Step 1 · declare a default

Every page sets data-theme-default on <html>. This is the surface's intent, not the user's choice.

<html lang="en" data-theme-default="light">

Step 2 · read the stored choice

theme.js runs synchronously in <head>, before first paint, and reads localStorage["prepeet.theme"]. A stored light/dark wins over the page default.

theme = stored || data-theme-default

Step 3 · stamp the attribute

The resolved value is written to <html data-theme>. Because this happens in the head, there is no flash of the wrong theme.

root.setAttribute('data-theme', theme)

Deliberate omission: there is no "system" option. prefers-color-scheme is not consulted. A screening interview must look the same to the candidate taking it and to the reviewer reading it back, so the surface default wins until a human overrides it — and once they do, the override is global across the prototype, not per-page.

Building a toggle

Add data-theme-toggle to any button. theme.js delegates the click, flips the theme, and on every page load rewrites the button's aria-pressed, aria-label and title. Child elements marked data-icon-sun / data-icon-moon are shown and hidden for you; data-theme-label gets the text.

4 · Typography

Three families, loaded as one Google Fonts request in every page head. Each has exactly one job, and the jobs do not overlap.

Families

Figtree

--font-sans · 400 500 600 700

Everything. UI, body copy, labels, buttons, table cells, headings inside the app. A geometric sans with open apertures that stays legible at 11 px, which matters because --text-2xs carries table headers and metric labels.

Body sets font-feature-settings: "ss01", "cv05" — a single-storey a alternate and a straight-tailed l, which pulls apart l/1/I in candidate names and session ids.

Fraunces

--font-display · 400 500 · opsz 9–144

Marketing headlines and pull-quotes only. Never a UI label, never a table, never a button. It appears in the marketing hero, section heads, testimonial quotes and the dark auth panel quote.

Applied via .display, which also sets font-variation-settings: "opsz" 144, "SOFT" 30 — the high optical size is correct at hero scale and wrong below about 24 px.

JetBrains

--font-mono · 400 500

Identifiers and machine values. Session ids, routes, timestamps in a transcript, class names in this document, .codeblock, .badge-mode, the OTP input and the live-interview timer.

Rule: if a human typed it, Figtree. If a machine generated it and a human might need to read it back character by character, JetBrains Mono. .mono sets it at 0.92em so it optically matches surrounding sans text.

7 · Motion

Three durations, two easings, and one rule that outranks both: motion is never required to understand state. Every animated transition in the product moves an element that is already legible at both ends. If you removed all animation, a screenshot at any moment would still tell you what is happening — which is exactly what a person with prefers-reduced-motion gets.

Duration and easing tokens

Durations

--dur-fast
120 ms — colour and border changes on hover, .btn, .nav-item, .chip. Fast enough to feel like a direct response.
--dur-base
180 ms — things that move a short distance: the switch thumb, the accordion chevron, sidebar width, theme change on <body>.
--dur-slow
320 ms — things that enter the screen: .dialog-panel, .drawer-panel, .toast, the sidebar sliding in, .progress width.

Two animations exceed all three deliberately: the score ring arc at 900 ms (the number counting up is the feedback moment) and the skeleton shimmer at 1.6 s (a slow loop reads as "waiting", a fast one as "broken").

Easings

--ease-out
cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) — the default. Fast start, long settle. Use for anything entering, expanding or responding to a click.
--ease-in-out
cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1) — symmetric. Only for looping or reversible motion: the indeterminate progress sweep and the live waveform bars.

Hover or focus the track to move the square (--dur-slow · --ease-out).

Reduced motion

Two independent mechanisms, both in base.css. The media query @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) honours the operating-system setting automatically. The attribute [data-motion="reduced"] on <html> is the in-product override, set by PrepeetTheme.setMotion('reduced') from the accessibility section of the settings screen and stored in localStorage["prepeet.motion"]. Both clamp every animation and transition to 0.01 ms and force animation-iteration-count: 1.

10 · Focus and keyboard

A screening interview can be a person's route into a job. It has to be completable without a mouse. Everything in this section is enforced globally in base.css so it is hard to opt out of by accident.

The focus ring

:focus { outline: none; } :focus-visible { outline: 2px solid transparent; box-shadow: var(--focus-ring); border-radius: var(--r-sm); } --focus-ring: 0 0 0 3px var(--focus-color); --focus-color: rgba(35,138,123,.45) /* light */ --focus-color: rgba(118,197,184,.55) /* dark */

Three px, translucent, brand-tinted. Translucency means the ring reads against a white card, a teal button and a dark interview stage without needing a per-component override.

:focus-visible, not :focus. Clicking a button does not ring it; tabbing to it does. That is the browser's heuristic and we do not fight it.

The transparent outline is load-bearing. In Windows High Contrast / forced-colors mode, box-shadow is dropped entirely; the 2 px transparent outline is repainted by the OS in the system highlight colour, so focus survives.

Known limitation: because the ring is a shadow rather than an outline, an ancestor with overflow: hidden clips it. .accordion, .table-wrap, .turn-eval and .dialog-panel all set it. Inside those, keep focusable elements a few pixels off the edge, or the ring is trimmed on one side.

11 · Charts and data visualisation

Prepeet's charts are small, categorical and always about counts or scores. There is no charting library in the prototype — .bars, .comp-bar, .progress and .ring are CSS, and anything more complex is inline SVG. That constraint is useful: it makes it hard to build a chart nobody can read.

Categorical palette, in usage order

Seven series. Use them in order — a two-series chart uses 1 and 2, never 1 and 5. The order is chosen so the first four are maximally distinguishable both by hue and by lightness, which is what makes them survive greyscale printing and the common forms of colour-vision deficiency. Series 3 (ember) and 7 (stone) are last because they fail 3:1 against a white surface; if you need them, add direct labels.

The accessible-chart pattern

Four parts, in this order, every time.

1 · role="img"

The chart container gets role="img" so assistive technology treats the pile of <span>s as one object rather than reading twelve empty elements.

2 · aria-label

A one-sentence label naming what the chart plots and over what period. Not "bar chart" — the role already says that.

3 · .chart-summary

A visible paragraph immediately after, stating the actual numbers and the takeaway. Visible, not .sr-only — sighted readers scanning quickly benefit most.

4 · optional table

When a reader might need exact values, add a <details> with a real .table. Required whenever the chart has more than about eight data points.

Rules

  • Every chart carries a text summary. .chart-summary is not optional and it is not "Figure 3". It states the numbers a reader would otherwise have to estimate from pixels, and it says what the chart is for.
  • Use the palette in order, and stop at four series without direct labels.
  • Never encode a category by colour alone. Position, direct label or legend order must also distinguish it.
  • Axes start at zero for counts. A truncated axis on a hiring metric is a misrepresentation, not a design choice.
  • Say what the denominator is. "57% of 72 reviews" not "57%".
  • Never chart a score without its confidence. A competency chart shows the confidence range; see the next section.

Uncertainty rules

Three rules. Breaking any one of them turns an evidence tool into a ranking machine, which is the failure mode this product exists to avoid.

1 · Never show a score without its confidence

Wherever a number appears, the evidence count, the confidence level or the range appears with it. Prepeet.confidenceMeta supplies the three labels and their one-line explanations — high ("multiple independent evidence spans support each competency score"), medium, low ("sparse evidence; treat scores as indicative only"). A bare 74 in a table cell is a bug.

15 · Content and accessibility checklist

The rules every screen is built against. The writing rules come first because most accessibility failures in a product like this one are failures of language, not of markup.

Writing rules

Candidate — supportive and direct

  • Name the single highest-value change, not six.
  • Describe the answer, never the person. “This answer skipped the diagnosis”, not “you are careless”.
  • Say what a score is and is not, every time you show one.
  • Give the next action a candidate can take tonight.

Recruiter — objective and evidence-first

  • Quote before you characterise; every claim links to a timestamp.
  • Frame differences as “worth clarifying”, never as a judgement of character.
  • Say “unverified”, and say that unverified does not mean untrue.
  • State plainly that the decision is the reviewer's.

Platform — concise and operational

  • Nouns, numbers, verbs. No adjectives in a status.
  • Every threshold names its objective and its current value.
  • Every privileged action names what it will do before it does it.
  • Silence is not success — degraded and partial data say so.
